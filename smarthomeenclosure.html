Temperature Sensor Enclosure

Solidworks · Mechanical Design · 3D Printing

We've been working on setting up Smart Home devices for our household. Typical implementations include automation of lights and fans, but we also wanted to build a device that we could use to measure the ambient temperature throughout the day. The temperature sensor is a prebuilt module which is connected to an ESP32 board, which needs to be powered. In order to mount the device and organize its components, we need to make an enclosure.

Discussion

The shell was modeled in Solidworks, and 3D printed with PLA. Heat set inserts were used for mounting hardware, and the part revision was printed onto itself for easy identification. On the top shell, there are four bolts which connect the two halves together. Their surfaces were offset from the top of the shell, so that standard length bolts would work with the design. Finally, a hexagonal perforation was added to the top shell to allow the sensor to measure the ambient temperature.

Once the bottom shell had reached a certain point, the top shell could be made using a top down assembly method. Top Down assembly makes it easy to resue geometry and create relations with other parts in your assembly. However, relationships between different parts can lead to complexitiy and strange errors. This can be avoided by breaking the relationships between parts. Once the relationship is broken, the part can be dimensioned as if it was created the usual way.

Future Work

In the future, I want to add a feature that will help center the two parts, like a lip that extends past the bottom of the shell (see below). Additionally, I want to move the perferation to a different face, so that the device can be mounted to a wall outside without fear of water damage from rain.
